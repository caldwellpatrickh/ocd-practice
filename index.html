5-minute meditation
This should feel mildly uncomfortable, not soothing. You're training: thought ≠ threat.

Ready

Press start when you're ready. Find a comfortable seat. This is not to eliminate thoughts — it's to change your relationship to them.

What you're doing in each minute
Minute 1Settle

Just sit. Notice your breath without changing it. Say internally: "This is not to eliminate thoughts. This is to change my relationship to them."

Minute 2Thought labeling

When a thought appears, mentally say "OCD thought." No arguing, correcting or reassuring. Label it and return to breath.

Minute 3Anxiety allowance

If anxiety rises, say internally "anxiety is allowed." Notice where it lives in your body. Observe it like weather. Don't try to solve it.

Minute 4Urge observation

If an urge to do a ritual appears, say "there is the urge," rate it 0–10 silently, and do nothing. Watch it rise, plateau and move.

Minute 5Reclaim authority

"Thoughts are not commands. Urges are not emergencies. I choose behavior, not OCD."

Remember

Don't use this to lower anxiety before an exposure. That's avoidance. Practice once daily, not during a panic spike.

Meditation is practice. ERP is treatment. Life is exposure.

About

OCD Daily Practice is a free tool for practicing exposure and response prevention (ERP) and mindfulness between therapy sessions. ERP means facing the things that trigger your OCD, a little at a time, without doing the ritual that usually follows. Over time, the anxiety and the urges lose their grip.

It works best alongside a therapist who treats OCD (see Find a therapist). It is not a medical device and does not replace professional treatment. If you're in crisis, call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, US).

Find a therapist

If you don't have a therapist yet, these directories are a good place to start. They're independent organizations, not connected to this tool.

  1. International OCD Foundation (IOCDF) Resource Directory: therapists, clinics and support groups that focus on OCD. Fewer listings, but more specialized.
  2. Psychology Today therapist finder: many more listings, with filters for location, insurance and online sessions. Therapists describe their own specialties.

Whichever you use, ask any therapist you contact whether they treat OCD with exposure and response prevention (ERP).

A daily routine

You don't need to use every tool every day. A simple rhythm:

  1. As it happens: log each triggering moment. Keep it short.
  2. Once a day: do the 5-minute meditation, at a calm time rather than during a spike.
  3. Once a day: try one step from your exposure ladder.
  4. When an urge hits: open Urge surfing and watch it for 3 minutes before doing anything.
  5. Once a week: set a slightly bigger facing-fear challenge, and look at Progress.

The tools

Log an incident

Record what triggered you, how intense it felt (SUDS: 0 is calm, 100 is the worst distress you can imagine), what ritual you did and how long it took. If you resisted, write "none." That's a win worth recording.

Recent entries and Progress

See your last 30 entries and your trends over the past week. Look for patterns over months, not days.

Meditate

A guided 5-minute practice in noticing thoughts without reacting to them. It's meant to feel slightly uncomfortable.

Urge surfing

Rate the urge, start the timer, and do nothing. Most urges rise, peak and fall on their own.

Exposure ladder

List situations you avoid, from easiest (Level 1) to hardest (Level 5). If you're working with a therapist, build this list together.

Weekly challenge

One exposure each week that stretches you a little past your current comfort level.

Sharing with a therapist

Your log can make sessions more useful. Your therapist can see what's triggering you, how intense it gets and whether rituals are taking less time. There are two ways to share it.

Download and send Easiest, no setup

  1. Go to Recent entries and click Download CSV.
  2. Email the file to your therapist, or open it in Excel, Numbers or Google Sheets to print it.
  3. Repeat before each session.

Share a live Google Sheet About 5 minutes to set up

Your therapist sees each entry as soon as you log it, without you sending anything.

  1. Connect a Google Sheet using the steps in the next section.
  2. Open your Sheet in Google Sheets and click Share (top right).
  3. Add your therapist's email and set their access to Viewer, so they can read but not change anything.
  4. Click Send.

Share the Sheet itself, not the Web app link that ends in /exec. That link is only for this tool, and it should stay private.

Connect a Google Sheet

This is optional. It sends each new entry to a Google Sheet in your own Google Drive, which is useful for sharing with a therapist or seeing your log on other devices. You'll need a Google account.

1
Copy the template. Open the template and click "Make a copy." It saves to your Google Drive.
2
Add the script. In your copy, click Extensions, then Apps Script. Delete what's there and paste this in (tap the box to select it all):
function doPost(e){const ss=SpreadsheetApp.getActiveSpreadsheet();const data=JSON.parse(e.postData.contents);const sheet=ss.getSheetByName(data.sheet||'Tracker');sheet.appendRow(data.row);return ContentService.createTextOutput(JSON.stringify({success:true})).setMimeType(ContentService.MimeType.JSON);}function doGet(e){const ss=SpreadsheetApp.getActiveSpreadsheet();const sheet=ss.getSheetByName(e.parameter.sheet||'Tracker');const rows=sheet.getDataRange().getDisplayValues();return ContentService.createTextOutput(JSON.stringify({rows})).setMimeType(ContentService.MimeType.JSON);}
Click the save icon.
3
Publish it. Click Deploy, then New deployment. Click the gear icon and choose "Web app." Set "Who has access" to "Anyone," then click Deploy.
4
Approve access. Google asks you to authorize the script, and may warn that the app isn't verified. That's expected, because it's a script only you are running. Click Advanced, then "Go to" the project, then Allow.
5
Copy the link. Google shows a Web app link ending in /exec. Copy it.
6
Connect it here. Open Settings, paste the link and click Connect Sheet.

Entries you logged before connecting stay in this browser and won't copy to the Sheet automatically. To move them, download a CSV and paste the rows into your Sheet.

Your privacy

There are no accounts, and nothing you write is sent to us. Entries are saved in your browser on this device. If you clear your browser data or switch devices, they won't come with you, so download a CSV now and then.

If you connect a Google Sheet, entries also go to that Sheet in your own Google Drive. Only you, and anyone you share the Sheet with, can see it. Keep your /exec link private, since anyone who has it can read your entries.
